# Choose a lens from the economic unit

Read only the relevant part. These questions guide research; they do not prescribe numerical assumptions, peer groups or a single forecast model.

## Semiconductors and other cyclical capacity businesses

Separate product segments and profit pools where the data permits: for example memory versus foundry, and premium versus commodity products. Examine price/mix, bits or units shipped, capacity, yield, utilization, customer concentration and competitive supply. Production capacity, production and shipments are different measures. A revenue residual called price/mix is not a measured ASP forecast.

Investigate cycle history and whether a structural change alters price, cost, reinvestment or risk. Compare the same products/periods where possible. Build or bound the transition to normal profitability, then explain what evidence favors it over a credible sustained-boom or adverse-supply alternative. Link sustained high profitability to capacity commitments, reinvestment and customer response. Do not put speculative product-level numbers into an apparently precise operating bridge when public disclosure cannot support the split.

## Automotive and other manufacturing groups

Separate industrial operations from captive finance, affiliates and minority ownership. Analyze units, geography, product/channel mix, realized price/incentives, FX, labor/material costs, warranty/recall exposures and capacity investments. Financing debt cannot be subtracted as though it funds only manufacturing. Transition economics may involve overlapping legacy/new capacity and regulatory effects; verify jurisdiction and timing before assigning costs.

Assess dealer/channel inventories, receivables and customer funding alongside reported sales. Track sales versus shipments and production. Margin normalization and capex cannot be chosen independently of the product/geography transition. Check whether royalties, affiliates or support obligations are counted in both industrial cash flow and separate investments.

## Banks, insurers and other regulated financial businesses

Establish the regulated entity, distributable capital and ownership perimeter. Investigate capital requirements, credit quality, reserve adequacy, funding, asset/liability duration and business-specific risk. Distinguish operating financial assets/debt from corporate excess cash. Generic manufacturing FCFF and a net-debt bridge may be unsuitable.

Select valuation methods with the domain author based on earnings, capital generation, distributions and regulation. Research adverse credit/claims/rate scenarios and their interaction with capital rather than merely shocking a discount rate. Requirements, buffers and legal distribution restrictions require current primary-source applicability verification.

## Other sectors

Derive a fit-for-purpose question set from the unit economics, competitive advantage, contractual cash flows and capital intensity. Do not force an unrelated business into these three lenses. Record why an industry source or benchmark actually applies to the selected economic unit.
