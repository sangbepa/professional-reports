# Board research profile

Apply the shared research evidence contract. Establish the document's purpose before selecting evidence: a decision paper prepared before a meeting, factual minutes of an actual meeting, or an explicitly fictional sample board book. These purposes require different claims and must not be blended.

## Frame the decision or factual record

For a premeeting decision paper, identify the decision requested, available alternatives, constraints, resource implications, risks and evidence that could change the recommendation. Build a decision-question graph around those issues. Record agenda items and proposed motions as proposals; neither placement on an agenda nor confident wording proves adoption.

For minutes, organize the question graph around what actually occurred: meeting identity, attendance, matters considered, documented discussion, motion status, votes and resulting actions. The research task is reconstruction from evidence, not creation of a plausible meeting narrative. For a fictional sample, record that all invented participants, dates, events and outcomes are illustrative; never attach real-meeting or approval claims to the sample.

## Establish event evidence and authority

Identify the legal entity, meeting date and type, jurisdiction and document status. Distinguish invited people, reported attendees and observed participation. Use supplied attendance records, recordings, transcripts, contemporaneous notes, minutes drafts and resolution records with precise locators or timestamps. An invitation list is not attendance evidence; a draft resolution is not an adopted resolution.

Record chair, attendees, roles, votes, abstentions, conflicts, recusals and participation changes only to the extent documented. Do not infer an unreported vote from a headcount or assume unanimous approval because no objection appears in incomplete notes. Distinguish a declared conflict from an independently established conflict, and a planned recusal from an observed one. Missing participants, discussion, vote details and motion status remain unknown.

If legality is requested, establish which current statutes and bylaws apply to this entity, jurisdiction and meeting. Record effective versions, relevant provisions and source dates. Research applicable authority, notice, quorum, voting, conflict and meeting-format requirements against the observed facts. Verify applicability rather than importing another jurisdiction's rules or treating a template as proof of compliance. State unresolved facts or legal applicability as material gaps; do not assume approval or validity.

## Preserve disputed and incomplete evidence

Keep original records distinguishable from later recollections, interpretations and drafted wording. Link factual claims to their supporting passages and flag discrepancies between records. For a decision paper, develop substantive alternatives and decision consequences. For minutes, alternatives concern competing interpretations of ambiguous evidence; they are not permission to invent alternative discussions or resolutions. Mark substantive policy alternatives as not applicable when the task is solely faithful recording.

A stronger recommendation, edited draft or subsequent action does not retrospectively establish what happened at the meeting. Preserve proposal, discussion, adoption, rejection, deferral and unknown status as distinct states.

## Deliver bounded research

Return the decision-question graph, source and claim records, alternatives and material gaps. Classify claims as observed facts, attributed statements, calculations, judgments or proposals. Explain which missing facts affect the decision, record fidelity or requested legality assessment, and what evidence would resolve them.

Prepare drafts and action-item records within the user's authorized local scope. Distinguish documented commitments from suggested follow-ups; proposed owners and dates are not assigned responsibilities. Do not message participants, request confirmations externally, convene meetings or introduce an approval workflow unless the user asks. Research should support an expert reader's decision or faithful record, without novice tutorials or fixed page and chart counts.
