# Earnings writing profile

Apply the shared writing argument contract to supplied research and reviewed calculations. Write for an experienced reader deciding what the results change. Every material analytic claim must trace evidence → calculation → judgment → reader action. Preserve the distinction between a supported conclusion and a question still awaiting evidence.

## Choose initiation or update

For new coverage, explain the business economics, relevant reporting basis, material drivers, estimate foundation and thesis tests necessary to assess the issuer. Establish the baseline without inventing previous forecasts, prior recommendations or a coverage history. Avoid generic lessons on financial statements or valuation mechanics.

For an issuer already covered, lead with the material change from the recorded prior view. Explain what changed in reported performance, forecast assumptions and thesis, and what remains unchanged. Use dated old-to-new estimates when available. If the prior forecast or thesis is absent, disclose that gap and limit the comparison; do not manufacture continuity.

## Make comparison claims precise

Identify fiscal period, release date and information cutoff distinctly. Keep issuer actual results, management guidance, recorded analyst forecasts and dated consensus separate in prose and exhibits. Describe management explanations as attributed statements, not verified business causes or actual outcomes.

Use beat/miss labels only with a comparable, dated consensus observation available before the release. Missing consensus means no beat/miss label. An analyst-model difference is a variance against that recorded forecast; a guidance difference is a comparison with management's stated guidance. Do not substitute either for consensus, or use a post-release consensus snapshot to imply a pre-release surprise.

Specify metric definitions, reported or adjusted basis, currency, units and periods where they affect interpretation. Preserve restatement and comparability qualifications. A percentage change, margin movement or earnings-per-share comparison must remain linked to its actual inputs and calculation record.

## Connect results to economics and decisions

Explain the supported volume, price, mix, currency, consolidation and cost effects that matter to the result. Distinguish arithmetic reconciliation from demonstrated causation. Assess recurrence with evidence and alternatives; do not turn an issuer's exceptional-item label into an independently verified normalization judgment.

Connect profitability to operating cash flow, working capital, noncash effects, investment and timing. Explain a material earnings/cash divergence without treating balance changes automatically as cash flows. Separate seasonal, transitional and persistent effects when the evidence supports those distinctions; otherwise retain uncertainty.

Present estimate revisions by relevant horizon, with recorded old and new values, changed assumptions and their effects. Keep historical actuals separate from forecast revisions. Explain which thesis propositions the evidence confirms, challenges or leaves open, and what would change the reader's next decision. Do not prescribe a rating or target price before the analysis, or invent a recommendation merely to complete the report format.

## Compose and check the argument

Use the research claim records to attach evidence and calculation references near material assertions. Explain the judgment connecting a measured change to its implication, then identify the reader action or unresolved decision. Alternatives and material gaps should qualify the relevant claim, rather than disappear into generic closing disclaimers. Assertive language cannot upgrade uncertain evidence.

Choose tables, bridges and charts when they clarify a material comparison or argument. Avoid repetitive metrics, novice tutorials, fixed length and chart quotas. Any separately supplied earnings tool must yield to explicit user structure and format constraints. Confirm that the final report preserves research scope and review status; writing does not establish financial truth or confer an unrequested approval.
