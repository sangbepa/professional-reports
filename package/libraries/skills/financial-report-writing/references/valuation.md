# Valuation writing profile

Write a valuation opinion or conditional analysis appropriate to the actual mandate, not a DCF tutorial. Establish the valued interest, basis, date, evidence access and the decision served, then lead with the judgment the work can support.

## Core argument

Make the forecast case recognizable as a view of the business: which earnings persist, which normalize, the capital required and the resulting cash. Show the evidence and selection rationale for the few assumptions that matter most. Keep observation-to-normalization and forecast-to-terminal transitions visible. Use a concise economic bridge or exhibit where useful; do not substitute a forecast grid for the argument.

Explain the economic units and chosen/rejected methods. An allocation of consolidated cash flow is not independent subsidiary SOTP. Cross-checks should expose disagreements and dependence, not provide decorative agreement. When DCF and market indications diverge, quantify the difference, identify the drivers, test credible interpretations and explain the effect on the adopted conclusion. Do not publish an unexplained weighted average.

Connect enterprise value to the interest valued once, with consistent ownership, dates and claims. Give the material bridge estimates their appropriate consequence and uncertainty. Distinguish point-in-time observations from conditional rollforwards, share-class economic rights from trading discounts, and standalone value from acquirer-specific synergies.

Present a range only on the basis supported by the analysis. Identify whether it represents selected valuation judgments or unweighted operating scenarios. Sensitivities and reverse DCF diagnose conditions; they are not probabilities or independent market forecasts. State adverse conditions that actually change the conclusion and evidence that would discriminate between them.

## Tone and content

Assume the board/IC reader knows what DCF and WACC are unless asked otherwise. Discuss why the selected inputs are suitable and where uncertainty changes the decision. Titles should identify the actual issue or finding, not repeat maxims such as “value depends on assumptions.” Put execution hashes, formula counts, skill names and quality checklists in supporting materials.

For a real valuation conclusion, precise calculation cannot compensate for an uncalibrated dominant assumption. When only conditional scenarios are justified, write that narrower conclusion clearly and produce the needed research return. Do not infer mispricing from a selected scenario's price gap before its economic premise is defensible.

On a content upgrade, return assumptions needing recalibration to `valuation-design` and `financial-report-research`; preserve previous versions, rerun dependent results and close the appropriate domain findings before handing the new reviewed content to `valuation-compose`/`report-design`.
