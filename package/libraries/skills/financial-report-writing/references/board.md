# Board writing profile

Apply the shared writing argument contract, then branch by purpose: an analytic decision memo prepared before a meeting, faithful minutes of an actual meeting, or an explicitly fictional sample board book. Identify the branch and draft status accurately. Expert prose must not blur the difference between a proposal and an adopted decision.

## Analytic decision memo

Lead with the decision requested and a supported recommendation when the research warrants one. Connect evidence → calculation → judgment → reader action for material claims. Explain alternatives, resource implications, risks, conditions and what would change the recommendation. Give the board enough evidence to choose without introductory tutorials or decorative analysis.

Keep management requests, agenda proposals and draft resolutions in prospective language. “The board is asked to approve” does not mean “the board approved.” More assertive prose cannot turn a proposal into an adopted motion. Preserve qualifications affecting authority, funding, timing and evidence sufficiency. Do not insert a resolution, vote or approval simply because a decision-paper template includes that field.

## Faithful minutes

Record only what the supplied meeting evidence establishes. Identify the legal entity, jurisdiction, meeting date and type, documented attendees and roles, matters considered, recorded discussion, motion status, votes, conflicts or recusals, and resulting actions to the extent known. An invitee is not automatically an attendee; silence in incomplete notes does not establish agreement or unanimity.

Condense discussion faithfully, attributing statements when the record supports attribution. Preserve documented reasoning without inventing participants, arguments, debate, consensus or legal conclusions. Use adopted-resolution wording only when adoption and the relevant wording are evidenced. Distinguish proposed, adopted, rejected, deferred and unknown outcomes. Unrecorded vote counts, abstentions and conflicts remain unknown rather than reconstructed to complete a table.

Analytic calculations or judgments belong in minutes only when they were actually reported or considered and that fact is documented. Otherwise place any requested analysis in a clearly separate memo. The shared argument chain supports analytic claims; it does not authorize adding the writer's reasoning to a factual meeting record.

If legality is requested, rely on researched applicability of current statutes and bylaws for the identified entity and jurisdiction, with relevant provisions and effective versions. State the evidentiary basis for notice, quorum, authority, voting or conflict conclusions. Do not assume legality or approval from a standard form. Missing facts and unresolved applicability must remain visible.

## Fictional sample and action records

Label a fictional sample prominently and consistently. Invented participants, discussions, votes and resolutions must remain explicitly illustrative; never present the sample as minutes of an actual meeting or an executed, signed or approved board record.

Separate actions recorded at the meeting from proposed drafting follow-ups. Preserve documented owners, deadlines and commitments; do not assign unrecorded responsibilities or infer adoption from an agenda. For a local draft, suggested owners or dates must be marked as proposed, and unknown assignments left unknown. Avoid invented signatures or approval metadata.

## Final writing checks

Trace material analytic claims to the research evidence, calculations and judgment; trace factual minutes to the actual event records. Confirm that editing has not strengthened a proposal into a resolution or an uncertain event into an observation. Keep material gaps close to the affected decision or record entry.

Respect the user's authorized local drafting scope. Do not send messages, seek external confirmations or add an approval workflow unless asked. Choose structure and exhibits for the document's purpose, without novice tutorials, fixed length, prescribed chart counts or unrequested presentation constraints.
